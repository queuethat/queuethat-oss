# 10DLC registration runbook (3-CG11, #336)

US carriers block text messages from unregistered numbers. QueueThat sends through **your own Twilio
account** (bring-your-own is the self-host default, so each business registers its own brand and
campaign). This page takes a business owner from nothing to an approved campaign.

> **No SMS is enabled for a US number until its campaign (or toll-free verification) is approved.**
> Until then keep the SMS channel off in provider settings (3-PV3, #338/#340). Web push and email are
> unaffected. Registration outcomes and timelines change: every fee and duration below is a
> "verify on Twilio's page" note, not a promise.

## Before you start

| You need | Notes |
|---|---|
| A Twilio account, upgraded from trial | Trial accounts cannot register. |
| Legal business name, address, EIN (US) | Must match IRS records exactly; mismatches are the top rejection reason. Sole proprietors without an EIN use the sole-proprietor path (lower throughput, verify limits). |
| A website or a page showing the business | The campaign reviewer visits it. |
| A phone number to send from | Local 10-digit number (10DLC) or a toll-free number. |
| Screenshots of the opt-in step | Generated by the join-page test: `docs/compliance/optin-screenshots/join-sms-optin.png`. |

## Choose: 10DLC or toll-free

- **10DLC (local number).** Best throughput and per-message price once approved. Two reviews: brand,
  then campaign. Typically days to a few weeks (verify). One-time brand and campaign fees plus a
  monthly campaign fee (verify on Twilio's pricing page).
- **Toll-free verification.** One review of one number. Simpler for low volume; typically days
  (verify). Same content requirements as below.

Pick toll-free if the business sends a few hundred messages a month or has no EIN-backed brand yet.

## 10DLC steps (Twilio console, Messaging > Regulatory Compliance > A2P 10DLC)

1. **Customer profile.** Business name, type, EIN, address, authorised contact. Wait for approval.
2. **Brand registration.** Register the brand against that profile. Standard brand for most
   businesses; verify the vetting score option if you need higher volume.
3. **Messaging Service.** Create one, add your sending number to its sender pool. Its SID (`MG...`)
   is what QueueThat sends through.
4. **Campaign.** Use case: **Customer Care** (or **Account Notifications**; pick one, do not mix in
   marketing). Fill in:
   - *Description:* guests who join a walk-in queue can choose to receive texts about their own visit
     (their place in line and when it is their turn). Nothing promotional is sent.
   - *Sample messages:* copy from the defaults below, with your business name.
   - *Opt-in method and proof:* the join-page checkbox. Attach `join-sms-optin.png`. The consent text
     is versioned and its hash stored with every consent; quote the exact wording below.
   - *Opt-out:* reply STOP. *Help:* reply HELP.
   - Confirm: no embedded phone numbers collected from third parties, no age-gated content.
5. Submit, then wait for approval. Fix and resubmit on rejection, using the reason Twilio gives.

## Text to paste into the campaign form

**Opt-in wording (version 1, shown unticked beside the phone field):**

> {Business name} will text you only about this visit: your place in line and when it is your turn.
> Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Questions: {contact}.

**Sample messages (the default templates; the business name comes from the location):**

1. `You're almost up (A042). Please head back now.`
2. Called: your ticket and counter, with the status link.
3. HELP reply: `Queue notifications from {business name}. Reply STOP to stop receiving them. Questions: {contact}`

Check the current templates in the admin template editor before pasting; the campaign must match what
is actually sent.

## STOP and HELP

- Twilio's Messaging Service answers the standard keywords itself. QueueThat also records them from
  the inbound webhook (3-CG8, #333) so it stops attempting sends and shows "opted out" to staff. Staff
  cannot override an opt-out; it is per business and per sender.
- START re-subscribes. HELP is answered with the business name and contact, at most once an hour per
  visit.
- **Open question for the pilot:** confirm against the current CTIA handbook whether a HELP from a
  number that already opted out may be answered. Record the answer here and adjust 3-CG8 if needed.

## Connect it to QueueThat

In **Admin > Providers > SMS** (3-PV3): paste the Account SID, Auth Token, the Messaging Service SID
and the campaign id, then turn the channel on **only after approval**. The sender is per business, so
each business repeats this with its own account.

## Hosted mode

If QueueThat is ever run as a hosted service, registering as an ISV with one brand per business is a
separate, undecided path. Out of scope here.
